# B4 Differences in errors

Use when relevant to the study's assumptions. Follow the shared principles in [study protocol](../../../study-protocol/SKILL.md).

Consider this when mistakes, unreliability, or error correction are part of the organisational explanation. Ask which errors matter and whether the construction permits them.

Possible evidence includes the kinds, frequency, and consequences of errors in relevant decisions or interactions. Compare with the source setting when evidence exists, while checking that the apparent error is not caused by a construction defect.

Fewer errors can change the role of an arrangement, including the headroom for improvement. Adding noise changes the model of behaviour and needs justification; restoring an outcome with noise does not by itself establish the human mechanism.
