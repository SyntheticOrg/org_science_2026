# C1 Behavioural variation and heterogeneity

Use when relevant to the study's assumptions. Follow the shared principles in [study protocol](../../../study-protocol/SKILL.md).

Consider this when differences among members matter to exploration, coordination, distributions, or collective outcomes. Ask which kinds of variation the explanation requires and how the construction represents them.

Possible evidence includes variation in decisions and interactions, comparison with a relevant population, or the behavioural consequences of assigned differences. Persona prompts, model mixtures, and other constructions need evidence for the role attributed to them.

Matching an average does not establish that relevant heterogeneity is represented. Reduced variation can also change aggregate effects, so it does not automatically make mean comparisons safe. State what was examined and how unresolved differences limit the argument.
