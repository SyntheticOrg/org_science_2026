# Study protocol

Use **Design -> Check -> Run -> Counterfactuals -> Interpret** as the shared workflow. The stages require explicit research decisions; their implementation depends on the question. [Design study](../design-study/SKILL.md) is the main entrance.

## Design

Establish the intended contribution and target: replication, counterfactual exploration, or both; representation of human members or investigation of AI organisations. Ground the design in available evidence and theory, and distinguish these from assumptions and departures.

Describe how member behaviour and interactions could produce the outcomes of interest, including plausible alternatives and unresolved questions. Consider the organisational features the argument needs. Roles, information, objectives, decision rights, and communication are prompts rather than a fixed model.

Keep a coherent plan of construction and evidence. Choose observations, comparisons, measurement, analysis units, and uncertainty appropriate to the design. When human benchmarks are absent, leave human correspondence untested.

## Check

Verify that the implementation represents the intended rules and assumptions before interpreting generated behaviour. Relevant checks may concern permitted information, valid actions, timing, constraints, or outcome calculations. Their form follows the construction.

Use [validity guidance](../validity-audit/SKILL.md) to examine consequential assumptions about members and interactions. Select proportionate observations, pilot work, or targeted diagnostics. Do not prescribe a particular probe, trait manipulation, sample size, or analysis method across all studies.

Distinguish properties assigned in prompts from behaviour demonstrated in the setting. If eliciting explanations or changing an architectural feature, consider how that choice may itself affect the process. Treat generated reasons as self-reports, not privileged evidence of cognition.

## Run

Carry out the chosen design with records linking observations to their implemented specification and configuration. Preserve failed attempts, departures, and consequential changes. Separate exploratory development and sensitivity analysis from the evaluation they inform.

When claiming held-out evaluation, specify the evidence reserved for it and protect it from adjustment. The bundled kit's non-focal calibration and freeze modes are one implementation of this principle; other constructions can keep equivalent records and separation.

## Counterfactuals

Formulate alternative organisational arrangements with a theoretical rationale. Explain the intended change, which features should remain comparable, and the assumptions the alternative preserves, changes, or introduces. Use the reference setting's evidence to identify uncertainties rather than assuming that correspondence automatically transfers to the alternative.

Revisit construction and checks where needed, execute the chosen comparisons, and examine outcomes and processes. Record which comparisons were planned in advance and which arose from earlier findings. Keep organisational counterfactuals distinguishable from sensitivity checks of implementation choices. A replication-only study can leave this step out of scope.

Counterfactual exploration can continue where human correspondence is weak or absent, with findings scoped to the model, setting, and assumptions examined. State which changed conditions extend beyond the evidence. A discrepancy labelled as a limitation does not validate extrapolation to humans; a failed synthetic replication alone does not establish a necessary human property.

## Interpret

Examine reference and any counterfactual findings against the available evidence, including competing explanations and uncertainty. A matching aggregate outcome alone does not establish a shared mechanism. Distinguish a potential threat, an observed deviation, and a boundary supported by evidence.

Use [reporting guidance](references/reporting.md) to document what was actually done. Technical details can be populated from records, while interpretation and unavailable facts require researcher judgement.

## Shared principles

1. **Make assumptions explicit:** Explain how consequential assumptions are represented and how their relevance could be examined.
2. **Verify construction and measurement:** Check that implemented behaviour, constraints, and measurements match the chosen design. Choose the independent unit from the design and account for dependence among observations.
3. **Keep adjustment and evaluation distinguishable:** Disclose which outcomes informed construction choices. Do not tune to an effect and later describe it as an untouched replication. Preserve a held-out comparison when making that claim.
4. **Retain the research record:** Keep the specification associated with reported observations, failed attempts, and reasons for changes. Never silently rewrite raw records.
5. **Match claims to evidence:** Report what is supported, what differs, and what remains unknown. Reporting transparency supports scrutiny without certifying validity.
