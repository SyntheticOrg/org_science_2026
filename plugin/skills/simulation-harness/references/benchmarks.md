# Using reference evidence

A replication starts from a source finding or protocol. Exploration can start from theory and available empirical evidence. Explain what the reference contributes to the question and what comparison it permits; a study does not need to use every kind of benchmark.

## Choose a meaningful correspondence target

Available evidence may concern outcome levels, treatment contrasts, variation, interaction processes, or qualitative patterns. Select the target that matters to the proposed explanation and the inference being made. A theoretical expectation provides a different kind of reference from observed human behaviour; keep that distinction explicit.

Published summaries may support useful comparisons without raw data. When replication data are available and permitted, retain their provenance and check how their observation units and measurements relate to the synthetic construction. Keep printed and recomputed values distinct.

Choose comparison criteria, uncertainty treatment, and the unit of analysis from the design. Do not infer equivalence from a non-significant difference or manufacture a numeric pass threshold where the evidence cannot justify one.

## Separate development and evaluation

Record which reference findings or outcomes informed construction choices. When claiming held-out evaluation, reserve appropriate evidence for that purpose and protect it from adjustment. If outcomes informed intervention selection, describe the resulting comparison as exploratory rather than an untouched validation. The kit's FOCAL setting is one implementation, described in [kit workflow](kit-workflow.md).

## Interpret the evidence available

An outcome match supports outcome correspondence. Relevant process evidence can strengthen an account of mechanism transferability, while alternative explanations remain relevant. Neither a matched mean nor a direction alone proves that the human mechanism operated.

If observations differ, describe the deviation and examine candidate explanations using evidence appropriate to the question. An intervention that changes behaviour and outcomes as predicted can strengthen an account without proving that the corresponding human property is necessary.

Counterfactual exploration can continue where human correspondence is weak or absent. Its propositions then concern the construction examined. Explain what the new setting changes beyond the evidence checked and what remains unresolved about transfer to humans.
